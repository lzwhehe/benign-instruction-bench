\begin{abstract}
LLM agents screen tool outputs with small prompt-injection detectors chosen by their scores on public benchmarks. We present a methodology for evaluating such detectors where agents use them and apply it to fifteen released detectors, including Meta's Prompt Guard~2, and two task-aware LLM judges. The methodology has three parts: an LLM-free replay of the ground-truth tool calls of two agent benchmarks, AgentDojo and $\tau$-bench, which yields benign-by-construction tool outputs and labels injected outputs by differential replay; an analysis of whether detector rankings transfer across benchmarks at a fixed low false-positive rate, with thresholds also calibrated on held-out data; and an audit of released detectors' training data. Rankings do not transfer from the BIPIA benchmark to AgentDojo: the best detector on BIPIA catches \ADPGTpr of AgentDojo injections at a 1\% false-positive rate. Between the two agent benchmarks they agree only partly; a detector that catches \MPRAD of AgentDojo injections catches \TBTprPR on $\tau$-bench. False-positive rates on tool outputs, which range from none to over 90\%, do transfer between the agent benchmarks. Where training data are public, their form explains the results: the BIPIA leader was trained on full BIPIA inputs, seeing attack strings as short prompts did not help it find them in tool outputs, and the best detector on both agent benchmarks was trained on agent-style inputs that share nothing with either benchmark. Deployment-oriented evaluations should use the agent's own tool outputs, report detection at a calibrated low false-positive rate, and audit the detector's training data.
\end{abstract}
